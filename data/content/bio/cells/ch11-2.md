# 11.2: Error Analysis & Standard Error
## SEM, 95% Confidence Intervals, and Data Reliability in Biology


            <div class="lesson-section">

                <div class="key-terms-box glass" style="border-left: 4px solid var(--accent-red); padding: 20px; margin-bottom: 30px; border-radius: var(--standard-radius);">
                    <h4 style="color: var(--accent-red); margin-bottom: 12px;"><i class="fas fa-key"></i> Key Terms</h4>
                    <div style="display: grid; grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr; gap: 8px; font-size: 0.9rem;">
                        <div><strong>Standard Deviation (SD)</strong> — Measures spread of individual data points from the mean.</div>
                        <div><strong>Standard Error of the Mean (SEM)</strong> — SD / √n; estimates how far the sample mean is likely from the true population mean.</div>
                        <div><strong>95% Confidence Interval</strong> — Mean ± 2×SEM; if error bars don't overlap, the difference is likely significant.</div>
                        <div><strong>Sample Size (n)</strong> — Larger n → smaller SEM → more precise estimate of the population mean.</div>
                    </div>
                </div>

                <div class="intuition-box glass" style="border-left-color: var(--accent-red);">
                    <h3><i class="fas fa-lightbulb"></i> Big Idea: How Confident Are You?</h3>
                    <p>In biology, every measurement has uncertainty. The <strong>standard error</strong> tells you how reliable your sample mean is. Small SEM = your estimate is precise. Large SEM = more data needed. The 95% confidence interval means: "If we repeated this experiment 100 times, 95 of those intervals would contain the true mean."</p>
                </div>

                <h2>I. Standard Deviation vs. Standard Error</h2>
                <ul style="line-height: 2;">
                    <li><strong>SD</strong> describes the spread of your raw data — how variable individual measurements are.</li>
                    <li><strong>SEM</strong> describes the precision of your mean — how close your sample mean is to the true mean.</li>
                    <li><strong>Formula:</strong> SEM = SD / √n</li>
                    <li>As sample size increases, SEM decreases (more precise estimate).</li>
                </ul>

                <h2>II. 95% Confidence Intervals</h2>
                <p>A 95% CI is calculated as:</p>
                <p style="text-align: center; font-size: 1.1rem; margin: 20px 0; font-family: 'JetBrains Mono', monospace;">
                    CI = Mean ± 2 × SEM
                </p>
                <p><strong>Interpreting error bars on graphs:</strong></p>
                <ul style="line-height: 2;">
                    <li>If 95% CI error bars of two groups <strong>do NOT overlap</strong> → likely significant difference.</li>
                    <li>If they <strong>overlap</strong> → probably not significant (but use a proper statistical test to confirm).</li>
                </ul>

                <div class="example-card glass" style="padding:20px; border-radius:var(--standard-radius); margin:25px 0; border-left:3px solid var(--accent-red);">
                    <h5 style="color: var(--accent-red); margin-bottom:8px;"><i class="fas fa-pencil-alt"></i> Worked Example: Calculating SEM</h5>
                    <p style="font-family:'JetBrains Mono', monospace; background:rgba(0,0,0,0.2); padding:10px; border-radius:8px; margin-bottom:12px;">You measure plant growth in 25 samples. Mean = 12.4 cm, SD = 3.0 cm. Calculate SEM and 95% CI.</p>
                    <details>
                        <summary style="font-size:0.85rem; color:var(--accent-red); font-weight:600; cursor:pointer;">View Solution</summary>
                        <div style="margin-top:10px; padding-left:15px; border-left:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1); line-height:2;">
                            <div><strong>Step 1:</strong> SEM = SD / √n = 3.0 / √25 = 3.0 / 5 = <strong>0.60</strong></div>
                            <div><strong>Step 2:</strong> 95% CI = 12.4 ± 2(0.60) = 12.4 ± 1.2</div>
                            <div style="margin-top:8px; color:var(--accent-red); font-style:italic;">Result: We are 95% confident the true mean lies between <strong>11.2 cm and 13.6 cm</strong>.</div>
                        </div>
                    </details>
                </div>

                <div class="glass-card" style="border-left: 4px solid var(--accent-red); padding: 20px; margin-top: 30px;">
                    <h4><i class="fas fa-clipboard-check"></i> Summary</h4>
                    <ul style="line-height: 2; font-size: 0.92rem;">
                        <li>SD measures data spread; SEM measures precision of the mean (SEM = SD/√n).</li>
                        <li>95% CI = Mean ± 2×SEM. Larger sample → smaller SEM → narrower CI.</li>
                        <li>Non-overlapping 95% CI error bars suggest statistically significant differences.</li>
                        <li>Always report SEM or CI when presenting biological data.</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>
