# 12.1: Designing the Lab
## Variables, Controls, Hypothesis Formation, and Experimental Rigor


            <div class="lesson-section">

                <div class="key-terms-box glass" style="border-left: 4px solid var(--accent-red); padding: 20px; margin-bottom: 30px; border-radius: var(--standard-radius);">
                    <h4 style="color: var(--accent-red); margin-bottom: 12px;"><i class="fas fa-key"></i> Key Terms</h4>
                    <div style="display: grid; grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr; gap: 8px; font-size: 0.9rem;">
                        <div><strong>Independent Variable (IV)</strong> — The variable deliberately manipulated by the experimenter.</div>
                        <div><strong>Dependent Variable (DV)</strong> — The variable measured in response to the IV.</div>
                        <div><strong>Control Group</strong> — Group that receives no treatment; used as a baseline for comparison.</div>
                        <div><strong>Constants</strong> — All variables kept the same across experimental and control groups to ensure a fair test.</div>
                        <div><strong>Hypothesis</strong> — A testable, falsifiable prediction: "If [IV], then [DV], because [reasoning]."</div>
                        <div><strong>Replication</strong> — Repeating the experiment with multiple trials/samples to increase statistical reliability.</div>
                    </div>
                </div>

                <div class="intuition-box glass" style="border-left-color: var(--accent-red);">
                    <h3><i class="fas fa-lightbulb"></i> Big Idea: The Goal Is to Eliminate Alternative Explanations</h3>
                    <p>A well-designed experiment isolates one variable at a time. If your plant growth experiment shows a difference, you need to prove it's because of the fertilizer (IV) and NOT because of sunlight, water, soil type, or temperature (constants). <strong>Good experimental design eliminates confounding variables</strong> so only the IV explains the DV.</p>
                </div>

                <h2>I. Anatomy of an Experiment</h2>
                <ol style="line-height: 2.2;">
                    <li><strong>Ask a question</strong> → based on observation.</li>
                    <li><strong>Research</strong> → find background information.</li>
                    <li><strong>Formulate a hypothesis</strong> → testable, falsifiable prediction.</li>
                    <li><strong>Design the experiment</strong> → identify IV, DV, constants, control group.</li>
                    <li><strong>Collect data</strong> → quantitative (numbers) preferred over qualitative (descriptions).</li>
                    <li><strong>Analyze data</strong> → use statistics (chi-square, SEM, 95% CI).</li>
                    <li><strong>Draw conclusions</strong> → support or reject the hypothesis based on evidence.</li>
                </ol>

                <h2>II. Common Experimental Design Errors</h2>
                <ul style="line-height: 2;">
                    <li><strong>No control group:</strong> Without a baseline, you can't attribute results to your IV.</li>
                    <li><strong>Small sample size:</strong> Results may be due to random variation, not the IV.</li>
                    <li><strong>Confounding variables:</strong> Uncontrolled variables that may influence the DV.</li>
                    <li><strong>Researcher bias:</strong> Using double-blind procedures eliminates this.</li>
                </ul>

                <div class="glass-card" style="border-left: 4px solid var(--accent-red); padding: 20px; margin-top: 30px;">
                    <h4><i class="fas fa-clipboard-check"></i> Summary</h4>
                    <ul style="line-height: 2; font-size: 0.92rem;">
                        <li>Experiments must have a clear IV, DV, constants, and control group.</li>
                        <li>Hypotheses must be testable and falsifiable.</li>
                        <li>Replication and adequate sample size increase reliability.</li>
                        <li>Confounding variables must be eliminated through careful experimental design.</li>
                    </ul>
