# 12.2: FRQ Writing Protocols (CER)
## Claim, Evidence, and Reasoning — The Scientific Writing Standard

            <div class="lesson-section">

                <div class="key-terms-box glass" style="border-left: 4px solid var(--accent-magenta); padding: 20px; margin-bottom: 30px; border-radius: var(--standard-radius);">
                    <h4 style="color: var(--accent-magenta); margin-bottom: 12px;"><i class="fas fa-key"></i> Key Concepts</h4>
                    <div style="display: grid; grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr; gap: 8px; font-size: 0.9rem;">
                        <div><strong>Claim</strong> — A statement that answers the central question or hypothesis.</div>
                        <div><strong>Evidence</strong> — Scientific data (numbers, trends, observations) that supports the claim.</div>
                        <div><strong>Reasoning</strong> — The logical connection between the evidence and the claim, citing biological principles.</div>
                        <div><strong>Action Verbs</strong> — Describe (state features), Explain (give 'how' or 'why'), Predict (state a likely outcome).</div>
                    </div>
                </div>

                <div class="intuition-box glass" style="border-left-color: var(--accent-magenta);">
                    <h3><i class="fas fa-lightbulb"></i> Big Idea: Don't Just State Facts — Build an Argument</h3>
                    <p>AP Biology FRQs are not vocabulary tests. They test your ability to <strong>synthesize</strong> information. Using the CER framework ensures that you don't just state what happened, but you explain <strong>why</strong> it happened using biological mechanisms. This is the difference between a 3 and a 5.</p>
                </div>

                <h2>I. The CER Framework</h2>
                <div class="glass-card" style="margin: 25px 0; border-left: 3px solid var(--accent-magenta);">
                    <h5 style="color: var(--accent-magenta); margin-bottom: 10px;">1. Claim</h5>
                    <p>A concise, direct answer. No "I think" or "Maybe". Just the facts.</p>
                    <h5 style="color: var(--accent-magenta); margin-top: 20px; margin-bottom: 10px;">2. Evidence</h5>
                    <p>Reference specific data points. "In Figure 1, the rate increased from 2.5 to 8.2..." Be precise.</p>
                    <h5 style="color: var(--accent-magenta); margin-top: 20px; margin-bottom: 10px;">3. Reasoning</h5>
                    <p>Connect the dots. "This occurred because the higher temperature increased molecular collisions, leading to more enzyme-substrate complexes."</p>
                </div>

                <div class="tj-edge glass">
                    <h4><i class="fas fa-bolt"></i> TJ Honors Insight: The "Predict and Justify" Combo</h4>
                    <p>IBET and AP exams love to ask you to <strong>predict</strong> the effect of a mutation or environmental change and then <strong>justify</strong> your prediction. Your justification <strong>must</strong> link back to the fundamental molecular mechanism. If a protein's shape changes, you must mention <em>why</em> (e.g., disrupted H-bonds) and <em>what</em> that prevents (e.g., active site binding).</p>
                </div>

                <div class="glass-card" style="border-left: 4px solid var(--accent-magenta); padding: 20px; margin-top: 30px;">
                    <h4><i class="fas fa-clipboard-check"></i> Summary</h4>
                    <ul style="line-height: 2; font-size: 0.92rem;">
                        <li>Use CER for all open-response questions.</li>
                        <li>Identify the Action Verb in the prompt (Describe vs. Explain).</li>
                        <li>Always include specific data when providing evidence.</li>
                        <li>Reasoning must cite a biological mechanism (e.g., Natural Selection, Thermodynamics, Central Dogma).</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>
            </div>
